# Hearthmere crowd motion · preview v2

## What changed and why

The previous resident view returned before matrix uploads whenever the 10 Hz / 2 Hz sampler had no new result. That froze translation, heading and leg bob between samples. In addition, the caller passed only causal.elapsed, which advances in quarter-second quanta, omitting the existing fractional accumulator. Neither issue is evidence of a slow GPU.

The authoritative town time is now causal.elapsed + causal.accumulator. Activity/detail selection retains its bounded 10 Hz near / 2 Hz far schedule. Visible bodies and articulation update on every actual render callback. A presentation-only clock bridges 60 Hz solo or roughly 4 Hz shared snapshots with at most 0.05 s / 0.3 s lead, then freezes stale input. Pause freezes pose and gait. Explicit startup, world replacement and dormant reentry rebase the display rather than interpolating a teleport. No presentation time is written into a save or gameplay ledger.

## Geometry, gait and activity

The immutable 100 IDs, names and roles are preserved. Household revision one updates home assignments and the residence dialogue while retaining the appearance recipe; see TOWN-HOUSEHOLDS.md. New deterministic appearance fields add body and shoulder width, leg ratio, four clothing silhouettes (tunic, apron, long coat, vest), five hair shapes, existing cap/brim/hood choices, and role-related packs, satchels or tools. Six shared geometry buckets and one shared material contain the articulated body parts; there are no 100 full scene-graph rigs or resident physics bodies. Seed 73129's full roster currently projects 1,716 active part instances / 25,156 triangles, under a 2,700 allocated-instance ceiling. Other seeds have slightly different active counts.

Thighs/shins use equal-length two-link IK; flat boots have explicit sole height and alternating stance/swing. Arms counter-swing. Seeded stride and swing settings, body breathing/bob, turn easing and continuously blended work/greeting windows differentiate people. The gait phase uses traveled distance and world-scaled, resident-stable stride, rather than dividing total distance by a changing speed-dependent length. Straight steady default-amplitude stance is distance-matched; start/stop blending, turning and contact sidesteps are stylized procedural animation, not full contact-aware motion capture.

The existing home/work/square routines already provide dwell, business visits and neighbor gatherings. Short waves and work gestures enrich those pauses. Nearby-detail budget selects full gestures; farther residents retain continuous walking and lighter idle behavior. Detail transitions blend. Named original quest workers and all economic/quest systems remain unchanged.

## Collision contract

TownCrowd is a shared seed + absolute-time kernel used by display, the solo worker and co-op authority. Canonical crowd spacing is observer-independent. The model uses anticipatory side offsets, 20 Hz cached offset endpoints, exact route positions between corners, a spatial hash, at most 16 neighbors per resident per pass, bounded passes, and conservative town-wall clearance. Final bounded feasible packing also checks interpolated-time and multi-player pinch points. It is soft kinematic crowd resolution, not a general rigid-body simulation. No global dynamic-body cost is added.

Player motion is swept along its entire segment against expanded resident circles, preserving tangential slide and outward escape. A 0.30 m resident contact radius is separate from the conservative 0.56 m wall-only silhouette margin. Actor context is height-filtered and stable-ID ordered. Town bodies are never added as steps, ceilings, combat blockers or authoritative save state. Solo applies the shared sweep before Rapier and rechecks wall-adjusted movement. Online applies it after the existing speed cap and before normal static/vertical validation; broadphase includes the whole attempted segment. All peers use the server's shared time and actor context, with bounded local prediction. Remote latency can still cause small corrections.

Display motion follows canonical targets continuously, ordinarily capped at 3 m/s, with pair separation and wall-safe tangential correction. Contact reactions are capped at 10 m/s to accommodate an explorer moving up to 8 m/s; no ordinary contact uses a packing-search teleport or automatic distance-based rebase. Pose speed and gait distance are recomputed from the final displayed movement. Extreme crowd configurations remain bounded soft resolution; diagnostics report unresolved actor contacts rather than claiming unconstrained hard-body guarantees.

Crowd construction and first canonical sampling occur before the worker's ready message without advancing gameplay. The existing first-pose material/mesh compilation includes every new shared bucket. Transactional world replacement restores the crowd clock/context on failure. Menus, startup, background pause, disabled lab worlds, and higher/lower vertical layers preserve their existing gates.

## V10 movement regression correction

The V9 life projection was not a continuous movement input: it held each 0.5-second life position while the renderer rushed to it and braked. The old numerical test supplied a new position every frame, hiding that cadence error. Ordinary same-home actions also routed through the road before returning to the same slot. Opposed travelers and departures through occupied service crowds could then become display-only jams. The previous distance-based recovery mistook those jams for reconnects and rebased all residents.

Motion revision 2 removes the unnecessary short clear-forecourt journey and safely simplifies an old saved journey on its next normal active step, preserving action, capacity reservation, supplies and position. Life-only rendering continuously replays the most recently completed half-second. This is an explicit bounded presentation delay, not extra gameplay time. Checked two-leg corners preserve walls. Fixed routines keep their prior clock path. Canonical life crowd projection now uses stable relative-velocity passing offsets; render contact handling includes stable departures past stationary users. Current explorer yielding is applied to buffered positions each frame. Backward corrections are removed only when the tangential alternative keeps actor/body/wall clearance. Reconnect rebasing requires an actual authority-clock interruption or an existing explicit lifecycle reset, never ordinary accumulated visual lag.

The life activity reaches a specific eating, washing, resting, leisure, gardening, water-drawing, cooking, crafting, maintenance or care pose. These are simple procedural gestures with grounded feet, not interiors or bespoke props. Many residents legitimately stay in one place while an activity runs. A workshop outage can now be addressed through public maintenance coordination at the square, with the same finite material costs.

## V13 frame-boundary continuity and honest diagnostics

The V12 integration still passed the gameplay frame cap (0.05 seconds) into the crowd. At 10 FPS the 3 m/s render correction became a 1.5 m/s wall-clock ceiling; at 5 FPS it became 0.75 m/s. Its half-second buffer also restarted when a snapshot was first observed, rather than at that snapshot's authority tick. Regular 15/5 FPS and jittered network arrivals therefore produced false braking/catch-up. The earlier 30/60/120 FPS tests did not exercise this main-call boundary. These are reproduced code defects; they are not a claim that they fully explain any particular device report.

Motion revision 4 uses `townFrameSeconds` only for town/frontier presentation. It retains up to 0.5 seconds of a visible callback gap, while ordinary gameplay/physics/authority caps stay unchanged. `TOWN_RENDER_TIMING` is a typed, fixed safety registry: 0.5-second authoritative endpoint spacing and ordinary playback delay, at most six historical endpoints, 0.05-second contact substeps, a 0.25-second online reserve, and at most 0.5 seconds of online presentation-clock lead. These are internal presentation limits, not new live tuning or simulation speed controls.

Each endpoint is stamped with its actual town-life half-second tick. The view ingests it once and samples the delayed timeline at intermediate substep times, emitting the final body matrices once per rendered frame. Root motion follows accepted target displacement and damps only the tracking/contact error; this avoids frame-duration-dependent speed changes from filtering a moving target. Existing ordinary 3 m/s correction, 10 m/s contact reaction, wall checks, body spacing, and actor exclusion remain in force. Timestamp interpolation does not extrapolate past the latest accepted route endpoint or invent a future turn.

Ordinary on-time online packets keep the 0.5-second delay. A real endpoint underrun can prime one additional 0.25-second reserve. Playback never rewinds; priming may create one brief hold, after which the reserve is retained until reset so packets do not repeatedly brake and catch up. This trades a bounded extra visual delay for continuity. Larger outages still hold accepted data. The current delay is exposed in the existing town view statistics.

A slow visible callback no longer triggers the old greater-than-one-second whole-population rebase. It advances through bounded contact steps and recovers over subsequent frames. Explicit initialization, world replacement, authority rewind, and dormant re-entry retain their lifecycle reset. A verified co-op paused/reconnecting → connected transition also retains the prior reconnect safeguard when the accepted authority clock jumps by more than one second; an ordinary slow frame or jittered packet cannot activate it. The short-resume case keeps its existing interpolation rather than forcing a needless replacement. Paused projections freeze roots and matrices. A long stall can temporarily leave a real authority/display gap; it is reported rather than hidden by teleporting.

Population check version 2 separates measured town render/authority/physics rates, actual root movement, changed matrix hashes, maximum lag, and persistent lagging resident IDs. More than 3 m of sampled lag over at least approximately 3 seconds is reported even when the displayed body keeps moving. Active callback stalls count as wall time; known pauses and absent-town rendering do not consume the live town window. Raw counters remain inspectable, but rate totals and motion continuity are segmented across known pauses/resets so a menu or hidden authority jump cannot inflate FPS or count a dormant rebase as walking. Undrawn authority fallback positions never count as observed walkers. Rolling history is bounded both to 15 seconds and 61 samples. Low render cadence is only diagnosed when town bodies were expected throughout the measurement window.

The historical `residentsAnimated` export field remains a compatibility alias for `bodyMatricesChanged`. A tiny breathing change can satisfy it. Neither field certifies visible animation, a bustling scene, GPU uploads, pixels, or adequate device performance. `residentsWalking` requires at least 0.2 m/s net root motion over the latest up-to-five-second span with a traveling activity. Camera movement counts use emitted torso positions, and an unmeasurable first sample is not a measured zero. Town → Record movement / view report retains manual game-canvas capture, Stop, Discard, JSON export and text fallback. Saves, resources, people, population size and all existing presets are unchanged.

New regression evidence exercises the actual main frame-time expression, serialized/reloaded old worlds, world tick acceptance, real Three instance arrays, 5/10/15 through 120 FPS, mixed callback intervals, online packet jitter, pause/deduplication, and 0.5/1/2/5-second active stalls. Source/emitted parity and the unchanged strict service-exit/contact tests remain separate gates. No real browser/GPU/device or automatic aesthetic approval is claimed. The same thirteen held files remain excluded.

## V14 one resident movement owner

All life snapshots, including an unchanged pre-navigation save, project their exact saved roots and carry an explicit movement-ownership marker independently of current clearance health. A temporary overlap during continuous legacy recovery cannot switch the town back to the old presentation solver. TownCrowd passes authoritative bodies through unchanged; normal navigated presentation replays accepted trajectories and derives gait from achieved root displacement. Fixed-routine laboratory behavior remains separate and retains its existing controls.

Main accepts life trajectories at the actual solo-worker and online snapshot boundaries, not only when a frame is drawn. Normal replay follows accepted corners and activity metadata. Main sends the next candidate replay roots to the physics worker, using the life clock. The scene commits the exact contact roots returned in a completed physics snapshot together with its explorer position, rather than showing a contact packet before it has been processed. This adds one bounded frame/worker handoff to the normal replay delay. The worker no longer constructs a second, differently packed set of bodies for navigated life. Moving residents are kinematic obstacles: the worker responds through bounded, swept, Rapier-clipped explorer motion, never by moving the NPC presentation away from its accepted path. Shared-world decisions and movement remain server-authoritative.

The automatic population report now reads actual emitted forearm matrices to measure hand-tip excursion relative to the body. At least 18 cm of excursion plus advancing same-task work is required for the working count. It separately reports walking, resting, waiting, quiet station residents, blocked resident IDs and camera-frustum activity. A changing hash, a need counter or a breathing offset alone cannot pass this check. Frustum inclusion still does not establish occlusion or GPU pixel visibility.

Verification includes the complete production main worker-message handler, real generated terrain readiness and acknowledgments, the actual Rapier worker's snapshots, world action acceptance and actual Three instance matrices.
